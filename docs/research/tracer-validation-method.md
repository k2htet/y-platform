# Tracer validation method: factual support and limits

Research date: 2026-09-30. Supports the founder-owned scope and validation decision in [issue #4](https://github.com/k2htet/y-platform/issues/4). This note records primary-source methodological guidance and explicitly marked inferences, not an accepted resolution. It does not authorize recruitment or sessions, revise issues #2/#3, or choose prototype implementation.

## Small qualitative rounds

GDS gives **4–8 participants as a typical range per round** for interviews, contextual research, experience mapping, and usability testing. It advises further rounds, with adjustment between rounds, when clearer findings are needed. [GDS — Plan user research for your service](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/plan-user-research-for-your-service).

**Limit:** This is practice guidance, not a statistical guarantee, a saturation threshold, or proof of population coverage. Six beginners is a founder-selectable practical target inside that range; the source does not establish that six will answer every question. It supplies no prescribed number for a separate practitioner realism review. Two practitioners would be a practical scope choice, not validated coverage of Myanmar support work.

## Moderated sessions and intervention

GDS describes observing participants attempt specific tasks, using believable goals and neutral instructions that do not reveal answers. Its usual session range is **30–60 minutes**, depending on task complexity. It advises mostly watching and listening, reassuring participants that the service is being tested, and asking follow-up questions about unclear observations. It also suggests think-aloud and allows occasional help when someone is completely stuck. [GDS — Using moderated usability testing](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-moderated-usability-testing).

**Application boundary:** The accepted choice to avoid continuous think-aloud, use short retrospective questions, and preserve product help/Guided Fallback instead of coaching is a tracer-specific decision. These sources do not establish that three Missions fit one appointment, that a fixed duration is appropriate for every participant, or that all intervention can be avoided. A practical appointment estimate needs checking once the throwaway materials exist.

## Access support and recruitment

GDS says to include people with limited digital skills, literacy constraints, disabilities, and support needs, and to ask about communication, assistive technology, usable materials, venue, and travel needs. It notes that some people with access needs do not identify as disabled. Recruitment methods and arrangements can introduce bias. [GDS — Finding participants for user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants).

For individual sessions, GDS advises allowing equipment setup, accommodating familiar assistive technology, asking before helping, and asking rather than guessing why someone acted or what they can do. [GDS — Running research sessions with disabled people](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/running-research-sessions-with-people-with-disabilities).

**Inference:** Access help should be documented separately from help with the work decision. A familiar input method can reduce a barrier without resolving a support judgment. An accommodation can also change what was observed, so record it rather than silently treating the interaction as unsupported. These guides do not establish Myanmar device distributions or participant-specific needs; see the existing [context note](tracer-validation-context.md).

## Consent, optional recording, and evidence

GDS's informed-consent guidance covers purpose, activities, data collected, use/sharing, retention, observers/recordings, voluntary participation, and withdrawal. Materials should use understandable language and an accessible format. [GDS — Getting informed consent for user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/getting-users-consent-for-research).

GDS recognizes notes as well as recordings and recommends recording observable behavior and what people say, separating observation from interpretation. [GDS — Taking notes and recording user research sessions](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/taking-notes-and-recording-user-research-sessions). It recommends collecting only necessary data, protecting it, and using it only within consent. [GDS — Managing user research data and participant privacy](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/managing-user-research-data-participant-privacy).

**Inference:** Optional recording with a notes-only alternative is feasible methodological planning, not a source requirement. It reduces one participation barrier but limits later replay and exact transcription. The UK guidance is not a finding about Myanmar legal obligations or an automatic retention policy for this project.

## Attributing barriers and interpreting findings

GDS recommends extracting what was actually seen or heard before grouping observations and interpreting findings. [GDS — Analyse a research session](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/analyse-a-research-session).

**Tracer-specific inference:** Preserve the original reply, relevant evidence inspected, committed action, visible consequence, participant explanation, connection state, help/Fallback use, and facilitator intervention. Ask neutral questions about unclear moments. If relevant, try a narrow access accommodation and record what changes; do not teach the correct support action. Distinguish possible work-judgment, language, input, interface, and connectivity barriers, allowing mixed or unresolved attribution. A retrospective explanation or improvement after help does not alone prove a cause.

**Conclusion boundary:** These methods can identify observed friction and tentative experience patterns in this scoped prototype. They do not establish career fit, capability, employability, population prevalence, production accessibility, reliable production connectivity, or real-job performance. Founder proceed/revise/unresolved criteria must remain explicit qualitative decisions rather than statistical pass marks.
