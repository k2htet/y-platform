# Tracer validation context: factual support and limits

Research date: 2026-09-30. Supports the founder-owned decision in [issue #3](https://github.com/k2htet/y-platform/issues/3). This note records evidence, not accepted product decisions. No existing research-note convention was visible, so this note uses `docs/research/`.

The tracer Career is not a public-launch Career; validation participants are not the final launch audience; validation assumptions are not production promises. Nothing here revises the accepted Career Simulation Experience Model from issue #2. The central qualitative, unscored question remains: “Does this feel like I temporarily did this job, rather than learned a lesson about this job?”

## Evidence relevant to the first frontier

### Career candidates expose different kinds of work

| Candidate | Verified occupational activities | Applicability limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer Support | Responding to questions and complaints, reviewing customer accounts, recording actions, and referring cases to supervisors; communication can include phone, email, text, live chat, or social media. | The source describes U.S. customer service representatives. It supports possible work interactions, not the prevalence of a Myanmar role or a particular workplace workflow. |
| Bookkeeping / accounting | Bookkeeping and accounting clerks calculate, classify, record, and verify financial data, including checking accuracy of transactions. | Bookkeeping clerks and accountants are distinct occupational categories. A small reconciliation slice does not establish that all accounting work is represented. Myanmar accounting practices require local grounding after the founder chooses the Career. |
| Digital Marketing | O*NET search marketing activities include analyzing web metrics, developing content strategies, implementing campaigns, and reporting campaign measures. | Search marketing is one part of digital marketing. The description is U.S.-based and does not establish Myanmar channel access, local work practices, or commercial demand. |

Sources: [U.S. Bureau of Labor Statistics — Customer Service Representatives](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/office-and-administrative-support/customer-service-representatives.htm), [BLS — Bookkeeping, Accounting, and Auditing Clerks](https://www.bls.gov/oes/2023/May/oes433031.htm), [O*NET — Search Marketing Strategists, updated 2026](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/13-1161.01).

**Inference, not evidence:** Customer Support can expose interpersonal judgment and consequences; bookkeeping can expose careful document inspection and discrepancies; digital marketing can expose audience/content/resource judgments and uncertain outcomes. All are plausible tracer candidates. Research does not select one or prove that one represents Myanmar youth's preferred Career.

### Participants and the conclusions a small study can support

The UK Government Digital Service's own user-research guidance says to recruit actual or likely users and specify recruitment criteria that answer the research questions. It advises considering age, gender, ethnicity, disability, socioeconomic status, limited digital skills, and literacy, and recognizes that recruitment method, place, time, and activity introduce bias. It gives 4–8 participants as a typical range for one interview/usability round, not a statistical representativeness threshold. [GDS — Finding participants for user research, updated 22 September 2026](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants).

**Inference:** Youth unfamiliar with the selected Career can speak to exploration and whether the work is understandable without prior occupational knowledge. Practitioners can identify unrealistic work, but their knowledge can hide obstacles novices encounter. Keeping these voices distinct makes their observations easier to interpret. Neither novices nor practitioners are automatically the future launch audience, and a small convenience sample does not support countrywide conclusions.

**Founder-choice boundary:** Sources do not select 18–24 versus 18–29, novices versus mixed experience, or one urban/peri-urban area versus broader recruitment. These are validation-scope decisions, resolved through the founder conversation rather than by this research. One reachable area can make recruitment and repeated observation practical; it also narrows what the results establish. Adding rural participants or diaspora participants introduces different access and work contexts that should be identified rather than pooled as interchangeable Myanmar youth.

### Adult-only versus including minors

GDS's own practice guidance requires understandable, voluntary informed participation. Its guidance for research involving children calls for consent from the participant and a parent, guardian, carer, or other responsible adult. This establishes that including children can add consent and coordination work under that methodology. It is **not** a statement of Myanmar law, a legal age determination, or a binding local procedure for Yway. [GDS — Getting informed consent for user research, updated 5 November 2018](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/getting-users-consent-for-research).

**Recommendation boundary:** Starting with adults can simplify the first research process. That does not establish that minors should be excluded from Yway or its final audience. If minors are chosen, local safeguarding and consent arrangements need to be established before recruitment. No Myanmar-specific legal conclusion was researched or made here.

## Evidence for later context questions

### Language

UNICEF's 9 December 2019 first-party account documents multiple languages/dialects in Chin State and a programme helping children whose mother tongue is not Myanmar learn the Myanmar language. This is evidence against treating Myanmar nationality as proof that Burmese is everyone's first language. It is a local education account, not a current nationwide literacy or English-proficiency survey. [UNICEF Myanmar — Mother tongue helps learning in Chin State](https://www.unicef.org/myanmar/stories/mother-tongue-helps-learning-chin-state).

**Unknown:** Current recruit-specific Burmese reading/writing, English familiarity, preferred work terminology, keyboard/input method, and literacy are not established. The selected cohort and workplace context should precede those assumptions. No universal English requirement or universal Burmese literacy claim is supported by this research.

### Connectivity and cost

Access Now and its #KeepItOn coalition documented at least 95 Myanmar internet shutdowns during 2025 in their report released on 31 March 2026. This is a historical documented-event count. It does not measure the probability, duration, or speed of an outage for a particular validation recruit or locality. [Access Now — Internet shutdowns in Asia Pacific in 2025](https://www.accessnow.org/press-release/one-region-half-the-worlds-shutdowns-internet-shutdowns-in-the-asia-pacific-in-2025/).

The World Bank's December 2025 Myanmar Economic Monitor describes power interruptions and infrastructure weaknesses affecting telecommunications, and reports a mobile data/voice low-consumption price basket of 1.5% of GNI per capita, above the selected regional comparators. Its cited ITU basket includes 70 voice minutes, 20 SMS, and 500 MB data. This aggregate comparator is neither a participant's current tariff nor the cost of using Yway. A June 2026 World Bank release still identifies power supply as an economic constraint despite some easing. [World Bank — December 2025 Myanmar Economic Monitor, printed p.22](https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099120625204042781/pdf/P507203-7c4662b6-c1d8-4c3c-9b0c-d4835f2763cb.pdf), [World Bank — June 2026 Economic Monitor release](https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/press-release/2026/06/16/myanmar-s-economy-shows-tentative-stabilization-but-fuel-shock-intensifies-pressures).

**Inference:** Interruptions and cost deserve explicit validation assumptions. This evidence does not decide offline behavior, guarantee access, mandate an implementation architecture, or establish current network conditions for the eventual cohort.

### Devices and form factor

**Explicit gap:** No current, representative primary-source distribution of Myanmar youth device ownership, smartphone OS versions, RAM, storage, display sizes, or personal versus shared access was verified in this bounded research. Do not infer these from aggregate connectivity measures, household phone ownership, U.S. workplace equipment, or national browser traffic.

The participant's validation device and the simulated workplace's real equipment are different questions. Selecting a phone for validation does not establish that the real Career is normally performed on a phone. Recruit-specific observation can establish the tested conditions without pretending they describe the country.

### Accessibility

GDS advises including people with disabilities, assistive technologies, limited digital skills, literacy constraints, and other support needs, and asking what help and equipment individuals need. This is methodological guidance, not a Myanmar disability-prevalence estimate or proof of accessibility. [GDS — Finding participants for user research](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/find-user-research-participants), [GDS — Running research sessions with disabled people](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/running-research-sessions-with-people-with-disabilities).

**Inference:** A fluent, digitally confident convenience sample cannot establish accessibility for other users. Check participant access needs rather than assuming them from age or location. Specific interaction and assistive-technology checks depend on the chosen Career, cohort, and form factor and can be framed at that later frontier.

## Customer Support context follow-up

Follow-up research on 2026-09-30, after the founder selected Customer Support as the tracer and primary participants aged 18–24 who are novices in one urban/peri-urban Myanmar area. These choices remain scoped validation decisions; this section adds factual support without recording a final resolution or choosing the workplace.

### First-party Myanmar workplace examples

**Online retail and order problems.** Shop Myanmar publishes return eligibility for damaged, defective, wrong, or incomplete deliveries, different return windows for Official Store versus other items, category exclusions, and warranty routes. Its refunds page distinguishes cancelled orders, returned items after successful quality checking, and failed deliveries whose refund process starts when the item reaches the seller. [Shop — Returns Policy](https://pages.shop.com.mm/wow/i/mm/help-pages/return-policy?hybrid=1), [Shop — Refunds Policy](https://www.shop.com.mm/wow/i/mm/help-pages/refunds-policy?hybrid=1).

**Limit:** These are company-published customer policies, not observed staff practice or evidence of a particular agent's permissions. They support the existence of order status, eligibility, return/QC, payment-method, and seller/delivery dependencies. They do not establish job prevalence or representative Myanmar policy. The [Shop Returns & Refunds FAQ](https://www.shop.com.mm/wow/i/mm/help-pages/returns-refunds-faq%3Fhybrid%3D1) gives different refund timings from the refunds-policy page. Do not silently combine them into a single verified policy or copy exact timings into the tracer. Retrieval of a policy page also does not independently verify current service availability.

**An urban local retail example.** City Mall's Burmese terms specify delivery to selected Yangon townships, distinguish returning goods at delivery from later requests, and use different rules by product category. A row for damaged/expired shelf-stable food explicitly names Customer Care as the decision-maker for a later exchange. Its site lists customer care through phone, Viber, and email. [City Mall — Terms and Conditions, sections 3–4](https://www.citymall.com.mm/citymall/my/login/register/termsandconditions), [City Mall — customer care links at page footer](https://www.citymall.com.mm/citymall/my/).

**Limit:** These are paraphrases of the published Burmese terms, not a verified internal workflow. The terms contain service-specific and potentially dated details; no update date was established. Institutional Customer Care authority does not establish that any frontline agent can independently authorize every exchange. This supports an urban retail example, not selection of Yangon for participant recruitment.

**Telecom/account/service support.** ATOM Myanmar lists Facebook, Messenger, Viber, and call-center channels for customer queries, requests, and complaints. MPT lists a customer-service hotline and self-service operations for SIM registration, balance, and purchased packages. [ATOM Myanmar — Contact](https://www.atom.com.mm/en/personal/contact), [MPT — Self-Service Numbers](https://mpt.com.mm/en/self-service-numbers/).

**Limit:** These sources establish real local service topics and channels, not access to internal account tools, compensation rights, escalation criteria, or the ability of support staff to repair network faults. The unrelated `atom.com` domain registrar was excluded; it is not ATOM Myanmar.

**Inference for comparing options:** A fictional domestic retailer can make order/delivery/policy inspection and explanations tangible without reproducing a live company's full rules. Telecom can expose account/package diagnosis and service uncertainty, but adds technical and privacy context. Both are grounded candidates. Exact customer records, queue priorities, staff authority, and handoffs need explicit fictional assumptions or local practitioner review after the workplace choice. These are not authored missions or production schemas.

### Burmese text and input

The Unicode Consortium explains that Myanmar Unicode encoding supports Burmese and other languages using the script; Zawgyi is a different, incompatible encoding. Matching fonts and text affects readable rendering. Unicode does not prescribe a keyboard layout, and keyboard arrangement alone does not establish the text encoding. [Unicode Consortium — Myanmar Scripts and Languages FAQ](https://www.unicode.org/faq/myanmar.html).

**Inference:** A participant's ability to read Burmese does not prove that entering an original Burmese support reply is comfortable on their device. Text readability, the participant's familiar keyboard, and short free-text entry merit observation if those interactions are selected. Unicode interoperability supports considering Unicode text, but this research does not choose an input implementation, require a keyboard installation, or establish contemporary Zawgyi prevalence.

### Device and accessibility follow-up

A bounded follow-up search of UNICEF and ITU primary sources found regional mobile-ownership indicators and global access discussion, but did not verify current Myanmar **18–24** urban/peri-urban personal/shared smartphone distributions or OS, RAM, storage, display, or keyboard distributions. The original gap remains. Do not substitute a regional statistic or a telecom's supported features for cohort ownership evidence.

W3C identifies small screens, touchscreens, input modalities, and use settings as mobile accessibility concerns; its current overview connects mobile to existing accessibility standards. [W3C WAI — Mobile Accessibility, updated 6 May 2025](https://www.w3.org/WAI/standards-guidelines/mobile/). Its keyboard guidance explains that keyboard interfaces also support people using speech or assistive technologies rather than a physical keyboard. [W3C — Understanding Keyboard Accessibility](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/keyboard).

**Inference:** A phone validation context should distinguish work judgment from reading, input, and access barriers. Recruit-specific observation can establish the device conditions actually tested. Neither a small cohort nor these guidelines prove population coverage or production accessibility. No additional locality-specific interruption rate was verified; the historical connectivity evidence above still requires locality/cohort limits.

## Open factual gaps after this note

- Realistic Myanmar workflow, terminology, authority limits, and work artifacts for the selected Career and workplace.
- Actual recruitment route and reachable participants for the founder's chosen geographic and age boundary.
- Cohort-specific language, device, input, access-needs, data-cost, electricity, and connection conditions.
- Local arrangements for consent and safeguarding if the founder includes minors.

This note supplies facts and limits for the decision conversation. It does not authorize recruitment, create a validation plan, accept a resolution, propose implementation tickets, or choose any production behavior.
